The season

One race.
A year to get ready for it.

Solar racing doesn't have a calendar full of weekends. There is one event that matters, it happens in the summer, and everything between now and then is preparation for it.

Stage one

Formula Sun Grand Prix

Three days on a closed road course, eight hours of green flag each day. Whoever completes the most laps wins — but the real purpose is qualification. A car that cannot survive twenty-four hours of track time does not get to attempt the cross-country event.

Stage two

American Solar Challenge

Eight days, four stages and roughly 1,550 miles of public highway between two cities, with timed checkpoints and overnight stage stops along the way. Cars run on whatever the sun gives them that day. Position is decided by distance covered, not by lap time.

Between now and then

The road to the start line

Most of a solar car season happens in a workshop and a spreadsheet. These are the gates every team has to clear before anyone is allowed to race.

01

Preliminary design

Mechanical and electrical concepts submitted for review, including how the driver cell protects the driver in a rollover.

02

Design reports

Full documentation across mechanical, electrical and solar collector, with structural calculations signed off by a reviewer outside the team.

03

Build review

Evidence that the car taking shape in the shop is the car that was approved on paper.

04

Scrutineering

Seven inspection stations plus the dynamic tests. Every one has to go green before the car turns a competitive lap.

From the shop

Laying up the array

Six square metres is the ceiling, so every cell has to earn its place on the deck. Encapsulation, string layout and how the array is bonded to the body all get decided together — a panel that delaminates on day three of the road event ends the season.
